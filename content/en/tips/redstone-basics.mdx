Redstone looks intimidating because it's a circuit hidden inside a block-based world. The good news: almost everything you'll build comes down to three rules.

## 1. Power has a source

Every signal starts somewhere — a lever, a button, a pressure plate, a daylight sensor. Power **does not flow from nothing.** If your contraption isn't activating, walk back to the source and ask: is this thing actually on?

```mcfunction
# common power sources
- lever         (toggle)
- button        (pulse)
- pressure plate (proximity)
- observer      (state change detector)
```

## 2. Signal weakens with distance

A redstone wire carries up to **15 blocks** before it dies. Past that, you need a **repeater** to top it back up.

<Callout type="info">
  Repeaters also add a delay — useful when timing two events apart, annoying when you forget you placed one.
</Callout>

## 3. Direction matters

Many components are directional:

- **Pistons** push the way they face.
- **Hoppers** point at the inventory they feed.
- **Observers** detect the block their face touches and emit a pulse out the back.

If your build is "almost working", the problem is usually a component facing the wrong way.

## Where to go next

Once these three rules feel automatic, you're ready for **clocks**, **sticky pistons**, and **comparator math**. We'll cover those in the [intermediate guide](/?category=tips).

<Callout type="tip">
  Build small. The most reliable way to learn redstone is to make a 3×3 contraption do one thing perfectly, then iterate. Don't start with an auto-farm.
</Callout>
